## Design Context

### Users
A broad mix of Canadian visitors evaluating a financial advisor: pre-retirees
researching retirement income strategy, ranchers and small-business owners with
no time for jargon, younger families deciding whether to consolidate planning,
and existing clients returning for tools/calendar/blog. They land on a phone
or desktop, often after seeing a referral or local search result, and they're
deciding within seconds whether this firm feels like one they'd trust with
their RRSP, kids' RESPs, or estate.

### Brand Personality
**Trustworthy, refined, clear.**

- *Trustworthy* is the table-stakes: this is someone's retirement.
- *Refined* (not "premium-luxury", not "minimal-tech") — Libre Baskerville
  serif headings + warm gold accent on midnight navy already lean editorial /
  private-bank. We commit to that direction with restraint.
- *Clear* — no jargon, no ten-bullet feature dumps, no animated walls of stat
  cards. Plain explanations and intentional space.

The voice is calm, never urgent. The site is read by someone making a slow
decision, not pushed into a checkout.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial / private-bank, executed with restraint.

- Existing palette is the source of truth: midnight navy `#0B1A2C`, gold
  `#D7C38A` / `#C6A667`, cream `#F5F7FA`. No new brand colors. Neutrals get a
  faint warm tint toward the midnight hue (chroma ~0.005–0.01) for cohesion.
- Typography: Libre Baskerville (already loaded) for display headings, Inter
  for body and UI. **Do not italicize accent words in headings** — the brand
  voice is consistent and the same font/weight throughout each heading. Use
  size, weight, color (gold accent) and space for hierarchy instead.
- Theme: light-default for marketing pages (financial advisory consumed during
  considered, daytime decisions), dark for the hero band and dark CTA sections
  (the existing pattern). Don't flip everything to dark.
- Motion: brief and exponential (ease-out-quart/quint), 200–350ms. No bounce,
  no elastic. One choreographed entrance per section, not micro-interactions
  on every element. Always respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Density: more space, fewer elements per row. Generous vertical rhythm. Stop
  filling every section with a 4-card grid.

### Anti-References (what to avoid)
- Fintech / SaaS aesthetics: cyan-on-dark gradients, neon accents, glassmorph
  cards stacked on glassmorph cards.
- Cliché financial-advisor stock photo over a navy gradient with "Plan for
  your future" centered on top.
- AI design tells already flagged in the impeccable skill: side-stripe
  borders, gradient text, blur-card-on-blur-card, identical 3-up icon grids.

### Design Principles
1. **Brand-locked, content-first.** Colors and content (no invented
   credentials, awards, or guarantees) come from the existing site. Visual
   choices serve the content; never the other way around.
2. **Editorial restraint.** Heavy on type and space. Light on effects. One
   intentional accent per section (an italic word, a hairline rule, a
   gold-tinted number) — not three.
3. **Performance is design.** A heavy-blur animated background that janks on
   a phone is a worse design than a static gradient. Phase 1 already gated
   ambient animation off coarse-pointer devices; design decisions in Phase 2/3
   keep that contract.
4. **One choreographed entrance per section.** Stagger reveals on the
   defining elements (heading, then body, then cards). No parallax, no
   per-card spring — they're noise.
5. **Respect reduced-motion and large-text users.** Touch targets ≥44px,
   focus rings visible (never `outline: none` without a replacement),
   contrast ≥4.5:1 on body text in both light and dark surfaces.
